# LinkedIn rules the writer and verifier enforce (2026)

- Hook first: line 1 must carry the point in under 140 characters (mobile
  preview cut) and never more than 210 (desktop). Then a blank line.
- Length: 900-1600 characters is the sweet spot for text posts; under 400
  reads as a throwaway; 3000 is the hard limit.
- One idea per post. Concrete detail from the source over general statements.
- No external link in the body — it goes in the first comment with the
  source name. Do not write "link in comments"; just put it there.
- Hashtags: 0-3, specific to the topic, at the very end.
- Plain text. No markdown, no headings, no bold markers. Line breaks are the
  only formatting. Lists only when the angle is genuinely a list.
- Emojis: 0-2, only where they add meaning.
- No engagement bait: no "agree?", "thoughts?", "repost", "tag someone",
  "follow for more", "comment YES".
- Attribute claims: "OpenAI says", "according to the paper", "the hospital
  reports". A vendor's number is their number, not a fact of nature.
- Timing honesty: only say "today", "just launched", "new" when the source
  date supports it. An older piece is discussed as a dated argument.
- Generic AI-sounding text is deprioritised by LinkedIn's 2026 authenticity
  update: a clear personal position and specific details are what reach.
- Banned phrases: game changer, revolutionise, unlock the power, next big
  thing, cutting-edge, seamless, transformative, in today's world, the future
  is here, AI is changing everything, disrupt every industry, leverage,
  harness, robust, paradigm shift, landscape, delve, dive in, deep dive,
  supercharge, elevate, testament, underscore, buckle up, let that sink in.
- Banned patterns: "It's not just X, it's Y"; "The real X isn't Y, it's Z";
  "Here's the thing"; rule-of-three lists for their own sake; "At the end of
  the day"; "Ultimately,"; press-release openers ("In a major development").
